how do machines learn to think?

no magic, no ghosts. strip away the hype and an ai is a very large, very patient machine for finding patterns. nine parts — training, the maths, and the quirks that make it feel alive.

01 · training

an ai is not programmed with rules. it is fed staggering amounts of text — books, code, conversations — and learns the statistical shape of language within them. no one writes the rules; the data writes them.

transformers don't read one word at a time. an attention mechanism weighs every word against every other, deciding which parts of the context most matter for what comes next. that is the real engine of modern ai.

🎲

the model outputs probabilities for the next token, not a single word. turn the temperature up and it samples more freely — vivid, risky, sometimes wrong. turn it down and it plays safe and dull. an ai's "imagination" is literally a randomness dial.

a model that only predicts the next likely word will sometimes invent one with total confidence. it has no way to check against reality. that is why your source graph here insists on real, shared facts — the fix for hallucination is grounding, not more tokens.

the anatomy of a transformer

a model is not one thing — it is a pipeline. each stage hands its work to the next. here is what happens between "you type" and "it answers".

the pipeline, front to back

your sentence is broken into tokens, pushed through layers that keep storing meaning, until the model is confident about the next one. it repeats until it decides to stop.

  • tokenisation chops text into digestible chunks.
  • embeddings turn each token into a pile of numbers.
  • self-attention lets every token consult every other.
  • feed-forward layers add non-linearity and depth.
  • softmax turns scores into probabilities.

the pieces that make it work

the vocabulary, decoded. click a term to unfold it. these are the same ideas the graph above will start drawing for you.

token +

text is split into tokens — small chunks of characters the model can reason over. "creativity" might be one token or several. predicting well means choosing good tokens, one at a time, until the sentence ends.

embedding +

each token becomes a vector — a list of numbers. words with similar meanings end up with similar vectors, so the model can do arithmetic on meaning itself. this is where the model's whole "world" lives.

attention +

a mechanism where each token asks how relevant every other token is, and mixes their information accordingly. it is why a model can connect the word at position one to the word at position two hundred.

self-attention +

attention where a token attends to the whole sequence, including itself. repeated across many layers, it lets the model build a rich representation of context before guessing the next token.

weights +

the learnable numbers that, multiplied and added through layers, transform inputs into predictions. training is the slow process of finding weights that make the model consistently right.

backpropagation +

from the greek, "backwards propagation of error". the model measures how wrong it was, then pushes that blame backwards through every weight, nudging each one toward a better answer. the heart of learning.

gradient descent +

the optimisation that follows the downhill slope of the error. like rolling a ball down a hill of mistakes, the model keeps moving in the direction that reduces error most steeply, step by careful step.

overfitting +

when the model memorises the training data instead of generalising, it becomes great at old examples and terrible at new ones. the ai equivalent of a student who learns the answers, not the subject.

parameter +

one individual weight in the model. a "large language model" is large precisely because it has billions of these. think of parameters as the model's neurons, and edges as the connections between them.

hallucination +

when the model confidently predicts something false. because it only predicts plausible text, not verified truth, it can invent fluently. a reminder that fluency is not the same as knowing.

temperature +

a dial on how randomly the model picks the next token. low temperature: safe, repetitive, predictable. high temperature: wild, creative, occasionally wrong. creativity and accuracy trade off against each other.

context window +

the amount of text the model can keep in mind at once. bigger context means it can weave together more threads — like a longer conversation, or a graph that keeps every fact you add alive.

go deeper

the glossy summary is over. this is the stuff under the hood — what the big words actually mean, and what people get wrong.

📏

scaling laws

performance grows predictably with three dials: more parameters, more data, and more compute. there is a surprisingly clean mathematical relationship — double the size, fix the data, and quality climbs on a smooth curve. that is why models keep getting bigger: it is not hype, it is a measurable trend.

🧮

embedding space

a word's embedding is its address in a high-dimensional map of meaning. nearby words are similar words; arithmetic even works — famously, "king − man + woman ≈ queen". the model never sees symbols, only these coordinates, and all reasoning happens as motion through them.

🌊

gradient flow

backprop sends a tiny correction backwards through every layer. in deep networks the signal can die out (vanishing gradients) or explode — so training is as much about plumbing as about maths. normalisation layers and careful initialisation exist to keep the gradient flowing.

🧭

alignment (rhlf)

after pretraining, models get a final polish so they answer helpfully and refuse harm. humans rate many responses; a reward model learns what "good" means; the policy is fine-tuned to chase it. this is why a raw predictor feels like a helpful assistant.

🎲

sampling & temperature

the model only outputs probabilities — a human or a sampling strategy turns them into words. pick the argmax and you get dull, safe text; sample with high temperature and you get novelty and mistakes. creativity in ai is literally a randomness dial.

🚫

myth vs reality

myth: the model "understands". reality: it predicts patterns with no ground truth. myth: it has a memory of your data. reality: weights are compressed shapes, not files. myth: bigger always means better. reality: it also means more cost, more energy, more hallucinations to police.
